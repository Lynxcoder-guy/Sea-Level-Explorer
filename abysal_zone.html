Abysal Zone

The abyssal zone (4,000-6,000m) is Earth's largest habitat—cold, pitch-black, and under crushing pressure where life moves in slow motion. Sea cucumbers dominate, crawling across vast sediment plains vacuuming marine snow (organic rain from above), while brittle stars and sea urchins scavenge silently. Amphipods (deep-sea shrimp) swarm in dense colonies, and rare foraminifera (giant single-celled organisms) build intricate shells the size of dinner plates.

This barren expanse covers half the seafloor, sparse but resilient life adapted to 2°C temperatures and food scarcity. Riftia tubeworms (near hydrothermal vents) and zombie worms boring into whale bones add bizarre touches to this desolate world, recycling nutrients in total darkness.
